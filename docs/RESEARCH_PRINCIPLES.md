# Research principles

Seven rules, in the order they apply. Each one forbids something specific; that is the only
reason to state it.

## 1. Mathematical definitions before biological analogies

An analogy explains nothing and cannot be wrong. A definition can be used, checked, and
falsified.

**Forbids:** "the network forms a concept", "the pool represents the stimulus", "this
resembles attention", offered without a measurable definition of the word. If you cannot say
what would count as not having it, you have not said anything.

## 2. Falsifiable hypotheses before experiments

The hypothesis comes with a reading that would refute it, written before the run.

**Forbids:** running first and deciding afterwards what the result means. Also forbids
"exploratory" rounds that are later described as confirmatory.

## 3. Measurement validation before interpretation

An instrument that has never returned a negative has not been shown to work. Break the
mechanism on purpose; confirm the reading moves; confirm the control arm lands where the
null predicts.

**Forbids:** quoting a number from a readout whose failure mode has not been tested. This is
the rule that catches the most expensive class of error — a measurement that is real,
reproducible, and measures something else.

## 4. Preregistered criteria before execution

Threshold, arms, `n`, MDES and the forbidden wording, in a file, before the run.

**Forbids:** adjusting a threshold to the data; choosing the readout moment after seeing the
result; removing a seed that behaved badly.

## 5. Reproducibility before performance claims

A number that cannot be reproduced from a recorded configuration, seed set, and code version
is not a result.

**Forbids:** citing a run whose configuration was not recorded, and summarising a result in
terms the underlying record does not support.

## 6. Explicit distinction between negative, undecided, and invalid results

Three different things, and only one of them is about the hypothesis.

**Forbids:** writing "no effect" for an undecided round, and writing "no effect" for a round
whose instrument was broken. A void round speaks in neither direction.

## 7. Historical records are preserved rather than silently rewritten

A decision is appended to, never edited. A corrected reading is a new record that cites the
old one. A roadmap change does not overwrite a verdict.

**Forbids:** making the archive consistent with the current story. The archive exists to
disagree with you.

---

## Two derived rules that are easy to forget

**Distinguish a world assumption from an emergence claim.** Wiring that the experimenter
assigned is an assumption and may only be described as maintaining a prior. A structure the
system produced under a stated rule may be described as emergent. Mixing the two converts a
design decision into a finding.

**A knob that was never engaged cannot support a null.** Before using a parameter as an
experimental variable, turn it to an extreme and confirm that an independent quantity moves.
Otherwise the null is about the knob's wiring, not about the mechanism.
